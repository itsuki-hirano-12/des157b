Annotated Bibliography

Lazy Fluency. Why It's Impossible to Translate Japanese. 2 October 2024. YouTube, https://youtu.be/WlPZZI_uLII?si=iQe1RvljMTNbpscu.

This is a video more directly about translating Japanese, discussing what makes the language so hard to translate into English. This is more casual than a scholarly article, but goes into the small details of Japanese language, and also discusses the topic purely on the perspective of translation. There are also great media examples of translations and localizations which are helpful to look at.

Matsumoto, Yoshiko, and Shigeko Okamoto. “The Construction of the Japanese Language and Culture in Teaching Japanese as a Foreign Language.” Japanese Language and Literature, vol. 37, no. 1, 2003, pp. 27-48.

This article is about Japanese education and focuses on the contents of Japanese culture and forms of the Japanese language in social contexts. The authors discuss how some parts of Japanese like dialects are simplified and ignored in Japanese language education. The article also offers some examples of the different forms of Japanese and how they are translated into English which are helpful.

Siegal, Meryl, and Shigeko Okamoto. “Toward Reconceptualizing the Teaching and Learning of Gendered Speech Styles in Japanese as a Foreign Language.” Japanese Language and Literature, vol. 37, no. 1, 2003, pp. 49-66.

This article focuses on how speech differences between genders are represented in Japanese textbooks. The purpose of the article is to discuss this representation for Japanese teachers, and how it should be taught in classrooms. Although the purpose of the article is not necessarily relevant to the project, the examples of gendered language and how they are translated into English give good insight. Some of the examples in this article feel exaggerated compared to real world speech, so I would need to be careful to write more naturally than the language used in this article.
